## Target character

This is an honest but enjoyable practice clean. Notes should have a defined initial attack, a warm middle, and enough sustain to assess legato without distortion or compression artificially evening out the performance.

The amp remains clean when played firmly. A very small post-amplifier EQ correction reduces low-mid cloudiness and adds just enough upper-mid definition to expose fret noise, uneven picking, incomplete chord fretting, and unwanted ringing.

Compression is deliberately off. Uneven dynamics should remain audible so picked and legato notes can be compared accurately. The noise suppressor is also off so quiet notes and natural decay are not truncated.

The Room reverb is short, dark, and low in level. It gives the sound a little depth while keeping timing and muting mistakes exposed.

## Amplifier

| Parameter | Value |
| --- | ---: |
| Amp Type | Clean |
| Variation | Off |
| Gain | 38 |
| Volume | 75 |
| Bass | 46 |
| Middle | 56 |
| Treble | 55 |
| Presence | 48 |
| Contour | Off |
| Cabinet Resonance | Vintage |

Clean without Variation provides an open and predictable response. Gain 38 adds a little body while retaining sufficient headroom for hard-picked chords. If the bridge humbucker causes audible breakup, reduce Gain before changing the EQ.

The slightly elevated Middle keeps single notes full and makes legato satisfying without hiding their attack. Vintage Cabinet Resonance supplies natural warmth at low practice volume.

Use Master Volume and Power Control—not Channel Volume—to set physical loudness.

## Playing notes

- Start with the middle single coil for the most neutral balance of attack and body.
- Use the neck single coil when practising legato, bends, and vibrato, but confirm that weak notes remain audible.
- Use the bridge humbucker for alternate picking, palm muting, rhythmic accuracy, and exercises that require maximum attack definition.
- Keep the guitar controls fully open so changes in dynamics come from the hands.
- During scales, listen for equal volume and timing from every note rather than simply reaching the target tempo.
- Compare hammer-ons and pull-offs directly with picked notes. The legato notes should be deliberate and clear, but they do not need to be unnaturally identical.
- During chord practice, confirm that every intended string rings and that unused strings remain silent.
- Let notes decay fully during slow exercises. Buzzing, premature decay, and accidental muting are useful diagnostic information.
- Use a clean metronome or drum sound that does not compete with the guitar’s midrange.
- Avoid adding more reverb or compression after a difficult session; both can make inconsistent technique feel smoother than it is.

## Adjust it for your rig

### Too bright or sharp

1. Reduce Presence from 48 to approximately 43–45.
2. Change Channel EQ High Cut from 10.0 kHz to 8.0 kHz.
3. Reduce Treble from 55 to approximately 51–53.
4. Leave the 2.0 kHz clarity boost unchanged initially.

### Too dark

1. Change High Cut from 10.0 kHz to 12.5 kHz or Flat.
2. Raise Presence by approximately 3 points.
3. Raise Treble by 2–3 points.
4. Confirm the guitar tone control is fully open.

### Too loose or bass-heavy

1. Reduce Bass from 46 to approximately 41–43.
2. Change Channel EQ Low Cut from 80 Hz to 100 Hz.
3. Raise the amplifier off a resonant floor.
4. Try Modern Cabinet Resonance if the low end remains indistinct.

### Too thin

1. Raise Bass by approximately 3 points.
2. Remove the 250 Hz cut by changing Low-Mid Gain from -1 dB to 0 dB.
3. Raise Middle from 56 to approximately 59.
4. Keep Cabinet Resonance on Vintage.

### Too sterile

1. Raise Gain from 38 to approximately 41–43.
2. Raise Middle by 2 points.
3. Increase Reverb Effect Level from 9 to 11.
4. Avoid adding compression until these simpler changes have been evaluated.

### Too forgiving or washed out

1. Reduce Reverb Effect Level from 9 to approximately 5–7.
2. Reduce Reverb Time from 1.4 seconds to approximately 1.0–1.2 seconds.
3. Reduce Gain from 38 to approximately 34–35.
4. Use the middle single coil rather than the neck pickup.

### Hard-picked chords break up

1. Reduce Gain from 38 to approximately 32–35.
2. Reduce guitar Volume slightly only if a deliberate volume-control exercise is intended.
3. With a high-output bridge humbucker, reduce Gain before lowering amplifier Volume.

### Legato notes are difficult to hear

1. Check that each hammer-on and pull-off is being articulated cleanly before changing the tone.
2. Raise Gain by no more than 2–3 points.
3. Try the neck single coil for a fuller response.
4. Raise amplifier Middle by approximately 2 points.
5. Keep the Compressor off so the patch continues to reveal uneven articulation.

### Too noisy

1. Move away from screens, power supplies, lighting dimmers, and other interference sources.
2. Confirm that cables and connections are secure.
3. If suppression is necessary, enable Noise Suppressor with Threshold around 18–22 and Release around 60.
4. Raise Threshold only until idle noise is controlled; confirm that quiet legato notes still decay naturally.

### Different pickups

- With a lower-output single coil, raise Gain by 2–4 points or increase Channel Volume slightly.
- With a very bright single coil, reduce Presence before lowering Treble.
- With a bridge humbucker, reduce Gain by approximately 3–5 points if chords begin to compress or break up.
- With a dark humbucker, raise Presence by 2–3 points before increasing the broad 2.0 kHz EQ boost.
