## Playing setup

| Setting | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Recommended pickup | Bridge humbucker throughout |
| Tuning | E standard, A=440 |
| Guitar controls | Volume 10; tone 8–10 |
| Technique | Light, controlled picking when clean; firm downstrokes and precise palm muting when distorted |

Keeping the bridge humbucker selected allows the transition to be made with one footswitch. If the clean part is too blunt, use the neck pickup for clean and return to the bridge pickup before engaging the pedal.

## Target character

The bypassed sound should be clean but not glassy: dark enough for the ominous passages, with clear individual notes and a little room ambience. Engaging the Metal Muff should add the entire distortion stage while retaining the punchy midrange and note clarity associated with the *Hardwired… to Self-Destruct* rhythm sound.

The pedal uses considerably less distortion and more midrange than a typical scooped Metal Muff setting. This reduces fizz, keeps palm-muted notes percussive, and stops the Katana’s speaker from becoming loose.

## Signal chain

```text
Guitar -> Metal Muff bypass/engaged -> Katana INPUT
       -> Clean Amp -> Noise Suppressor -> Room Reverb -> Speaker
```

Connect the Metal Muff to the normal **INPUT**, not POWER AMP IN or SEND/RETURN.

## External pedal — Metal Muff with Top Boost

Use clock positions as the initial settings:

| Control | Setting |
| --- | --- |
| Volume | 12:00, then adjust for a slight dirty-volume lift |
| Top Boost | 8:30 |
| Treble | 10:00 |
| Mid | 2:00 |
| Bass | 11:00 |
| Distortion | 10:30 |
| Top Boost footswitch | Off for rhythm |
| Bypass footswitch | Off for clean; On for distorted sections |

The Volume control is rig-dependent. Alternate between bypass and engaged modes, then set it so the distorted sound is approximately **1–2 dB louder** than the clean sound.

Do not start with the Metal Muff’s familiar heavily scooped setting. The elevated Mid control supplies the bark and note definition needed here. Top Boost emphasizes a narrow high-frequency band, so leaving it off prevents the rhythm sound becoming piercing.

For an optional lead lift, engage Top Boost with its control at only **8:30–9:00**. If that is still sharp, leave Top Boost off and raise the pedal Volume slightly instead.

## Operating method

1. Select the Katana patch and leave it unchanged.
2. Keep the Metal Muff’s **Top Boost off**.
3. Bypass the Metal Muff for the clean passages.
4. Press the Metal Muff **Bypass** footswitch for the heavy sections.
5. For a lead lift, optionally press its separate **Top Boost** footswitch.
6. Return Top Boost to off before resuming rhythm playing.

## Playing notes

- Pick the clean passages nearer the neck and use a lighter attack.
- For distorted rhythm, pick closer to the bridge and use firm downstrokes.
- Keep the palm mute controlled rather than completely choking the string; the riff needs weight as well as attack.
- Use less distortion than may initially seem necessary. The recorded sound contains layered guitars, whereas one guitar needs additional clarity and midrange.

## Adjust it for your rig

- **Too bright or fizzy:** Reduce Metal Muff Treble toward 9:30 first; then lower Katana Presence.
- **Too dark:** Raise Metal Muff Treble slightly before changing the amp.
- **Too loose or bass-heavy:** Lower Metal Muff Bass toward 10:00. Do not immediately remove amp mids.
- **Distortion sounds thin:** Raise pedal Mid slightly, then Bass in very small increments.
- **Not heavy enough:** Raise Distortion toward 11:00. Avoid going straight to maximum gain.
- **Clean sound is breaking up:** Reduce Katana Gain to 16–18 or slightly lower guitar volume.
- **Dirty sound jumps too loudly:** Reduce Metal Muff Volume; do not reduce Distortion to correct level matching.
- **Too noisy:** Reduce pedal Distortion first, then raise Noise Suppressor Threshold by 3–5 points.
- **Hot or active humbuckers:** Start with Distortion at 9:30–10:00.
- **Lower-output pickups:** Try Distortion at 11:00 and Volume just above unity.
